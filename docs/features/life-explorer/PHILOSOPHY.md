# LIFE EXPLORER — CANONICAL CURRICULUM SYSTEM

## Purpose

Life Explorer is a curiosity-driven homeschool system organized around real life.

The curriculum is not primarily organized by isolated school subjects. It is organized through the 12 Vibration Fit Life Categories:

1. Fun
2. Health
3. Travel
4. Love
5. Family
6. Social
7. Home
8. Work
9. Money
10. Stuff
11. Giving
12. Spirituality

Category keys in code: `fun`, `health`, `travel`, `love`, `family`, `social`, `home`, `work`, `money`, `stuff`, `giving`, `spirituality`.

A Life Category provides the broad context.

A specific topic becomes the current Expedition.

Example:

- Life Category: Travel
- Expedition: Antarctica

The Expedition provides meaningful context for reading, writing, mathematics, science, geography, art, communication, and life skills.

## Core Educational Philosophy

The purpose is not merely to transfer information.

The purpose is to help the child:

- Ask meaningful questions
- Investigate ideas
- Make observations
- Build foundational academic skills
- Create and communicate
- Reflect on learning
- Develop confidence and curiosity
- Connect education to real life

Curiosity directs the learning, but foundational skills remain intentionally supported.

## The Life Explorer Learning Cycle

Every Expedition follows this cycle:

1. **KNOW** — What does the child already believe or understand?
2. **WONDER** — What does the child genuinely want to know?
3. **INVESTIGATE** — Books, videos, podcasts, experiments, field experiences, conversations, maps, trusted websites.
4. **CREATE** — Draw, write, build, experiment, explain, act, cook, design, or present.
5. **REFLECT** — What did the child discover? What surprised them? What changed in their thinking?
6. **CHOOSE** — What would the child like to experience, create, practice, or explore next?
7. **CONTINUE** — Every answer may produce a new question.

## The Wonder Wall

Every Expedition maintains three living collections:

- What I Know
- What I Wonder
- What I Learned

Record the child's language as closely as possible.

Do not automatically correct an inaccurate statement in “What I Know.” Preserve it so the system can later document how the child's understanding changed.

## Parent Experience

The system must simplify the parent's life.

The parent should not need to:

- Search for activities during the lesson
- Open several disconnected guides
- Figure out experiment instructions
- Decide what questions to ask
- Hunt for links
- Translate broad ideas into a daily plan

The default parent experience is:

1. Open today's lesson.
2. Review a preparation checklist taking 5–10 minutes.
3. Gather the listed materials.
4. Open preselected links.
5. Follow the lesson in order.
6. Record brief observations.
7. Let the system recommend the next lesson.

## Lesson Design Principle

Structure belongs to the parent.
Curiosity belongs to the child.

The system provides a reliable academic and logistical framework while allowing the child's Wonder Wall questions to influence content and pacing.

## Pacing

Expeditions are not forced into arbitrary one-week or one-month limits.

The system may suggest a five-day starting path, but it must allow:

- Extending a topic when curiosity is high
- Skipping optional material
- Changing direction based on a new question
- Pausing for field trips or family life
- Continuing foundational skills consistently

The parent should never be told they are “behind” because the child explored one question deeply.
